## Key Highlights

*   **Regulatory Tailwinds:** Stricter EPR and BWMR frameworks are increasing domestic scrap availability, reducing competition from the unorganized sector, and creating a more favorable operating environment. Recommendations for strengthening EPR (linking GSTN, audit rules) expected by April 1, 2026.
*   **New Recycling Verticals:** Entry into high-growth areas like Lithium-ion batteries, rubber, paper, and steel recycling is expected to diversify revenue streams and enhance profitability.
*   **Operational Efficiency & Technology:** Continuous investment in automation and technology through an in-house project division aims to improve yields and reduce operational costs, maintaining global competitiveness.
*   **Arbitrage Opportunities:** Ability to capitalize on global price differences by importing raw materials from overseas facilities to India, leading to higher realizations.

*   **Capacity Expansion Delays:** Minor licensing delays from state government authorities impacted Q2/Q3 FY26, but expected to be resolved by Q4 FY26.
*   **Aluminum Volume Decline:** Higher metal prices led scrap aggregators to withhold material, temporarily tightening scrap availability and reducing processing volumes. Expected to normalize as prices stabilize.
*   **Aluminum Hedging Challenges:** Difficulty in hedging ADC-12 (not traded on exchanges) limits Indian aluminum recycling volumes; hedging options are being explored.
*   **New Labor Law Impact:** Anticipated annual impact of approximately Rs. 4.2 crores from recalculation of gratuity and leave encashment (FY27 onwards).
